The Airport Run

Everything I've learned about AVL from the driver's seat — including the one thing that surprises almost everybody.

← Back to all Asheville picks

Updated August 2026

I run to Asheville Regional most days. Somewhere in the range of a thousand trips at this point, at every hour, in every kind of weather, through two years of construction. This page is what I'd tell you if you got in my car and asked.

AVL is a moving target right now — there's a $400 million rebuild happening around the passengers, and things move. Everything here reflects late August 2026. Check flyavl.com for anything time-critical, and tell me if something on this page has gone stale.

Start here: pickup is not at the curb

This is the thing. If you remember nothing else from this page, remember this.

When you land at AVL and open Uber or Lyft, your driver cannot pick you up outside baggage claim. The ground transportation pickup lot sits at the north end of Shuttle Lot A, which is on Airport Road — Highway 280 — south of the terminal, across from the WNC Agricultural Center. It is not the curb, and it is not visible from the curb.

You get there one of two ways. Either you catch the airport shuttle, which picks up outside the ticket counter lobby and at the baggage claim entrance and runs continuously, or you walk down the sidewalk heading south from the terminal.

Every week I watch somebody stand at the curb with their bags, confused, watching their driver's dot sit motionless a quarter mile away. Meanwhile the driver's timer is running. Request your ride once you're heading to the lot — not while you're still at the carousel.

If somebody's picking you up personally: that's different. There's a free cell phone lot across from the airport entrance next to the shuttle lot. They have to stay with the vehicle. Text them when you have your bag, not when you land.

Hotel shuttles pick up across from the baggage claim entrance, on the far side of Terminal Drive near the parking garage.

When to actually leave for your flight

AVL is a small airport that is currently pretending to be a bigger one. It moved over 2.2 million passengers in 2025 through a terminal built in 1961, which is exactly why they're rebuilding it. TSA on a Tuesday at 10am is nothing. TSA at 5:30am on a Monday, or on a Sunday in October when everyone leaves at once, is a real line.

The exception is a midday flight midweek in a slow month. Then ninety minutes is genuinely fine and I'll tell you so.

The fog thing

This is the local knowledge that isn't in any airport guide.

The airport sits in the French Broad valley south of Asheville, and this region generates fog the way other places generate traffic. Cool, wet air pools in the valley overnight, especially in fall and spring, and especially after a rainy day. Some mornings you drive out of clear air in Asheville and descend into a wall of white around Fletcher.

Two consequences. The drive takes longer — that stretch of I-26 in dense fog is not a place to make up time. And AVL is a small airport where weather delays and diversions happen more than people expect. If you have a tight connection through Charlotte or Atlanta on a foggy October morning, know that going in.

If you want the reason this valley is like this, it's the same reason everything here is green: orographic rainfall, cool air draining downhill at night, and a river running through the middle of it. There's more on that in the geology guide.

What the construction means for you

AVL Forward is a $400 million rebuild that broke ground in August 2023 and runs into late 2027 or early 2028. It's turning a single-story, seven-gate terminal into a two-story, twelve-gate building roughly two and a half times the size.

What's already open and working:

  • The North Concourse and new TSA checkpoint opened in June 2025. All seven of its gates were operating by that July. This is the nice new part.
  • The first phase of the new baggage claim opened in April 2026. The rest, bringing it to three carousels, is expected in summer 2027.
  • The new rental car facility opened in May 2026, and this one catches people out. Counters are no longer where they used to be. You deplane at the North Concourse, go through the ticket lobby, and follow a marked outdoor pathway to the rental counters next to the new baggage claim. It's outdoors. In the rain, that matters.
  • Rental returns are in the parking garage, with key drop boxes in the garage and at baggage claim.

Still to come: five more gates on the south side in 2027, then the central checkpoint, ticketing and concession areas. Expect signage to change, expect a temporary route or two, and expect the walk from curb to gate to be longer than it looks on a map.

Parking, if you're driving yourself

Parking has been the tightest part of AVL for the past couple of years, and the airport has said as much. They added roughly 600 spaces in a lot near the WNC Ag Center, and a garage is in the plans but not built.

  • Check availability before you leave the house. The flyavl.com homepage has a live parking tool showing pricing, capacity, walking times, EV charging, and shuttle tracking by lot. Use it. This is the single easiest way to avoid a bad morning.
  • Parking is credit card only. No cash.
  • Holidays and peak fall weekends fill up. They open overflow areas and staff will wave you in, but that adds time you may not have budgeted.
  • Run the math on a long trip. Somewhere past four or five days, a round-trip ride usually beats daily parking, and you don't come home to a cold car at midnight. For a two-day trip, park.

Inside the terminal at 5am

Manage your expectations. This is not Atlanta.

The new North Concourse has a Jimmy John's, a Tropical Smoothie Cafe, a retail shop, and a couple of temporary concession spots, with a proper concessions court coming as part of the later phases. That's the whole picture. On an early departure, not everything will be open when you clear security.

My honest advice: eat before you come, or grab coffee on the way. If you're leaving from downtown or West Asheville early, there are places open at 5am that will do a better job than anything airside. That's a good use of the extra fifteen minutes you saved by leaving on time.

Landing at AVL: the arrival, in order

  1. You deplane at the North Concourse.
  2. Bags come out at the new baggage claim. It's a small airport — bags are usually quick, but a couple of larger aircraft landing together can back it up.
  3. Renting a car? Ticket lobby, then the marked outdoor pathway to the rental counters by baggage claim.
  4. Getting a ride? Shuttle or walk south to the ground transportation lot at the north end of Shuttle Lot A. Request the ride once you're moving that direction.
  5. Being picked up by a person? They're at the cell phone lot across from the entrance. Text when you have your bag.

From there it's roughly twenty to twenty-five minutes to downtown, a bit more to West Asheville, and fifteen or so down to Hendersonville. If you're heading straight to the Parkway or Brevard, you're actually closer from the airport than you would be from downtown.

Odds and ends worth knowing

  • The address is 61 Terminal Drive, Fletcher NC 28732 — I-26 Exit 40. Note it's Fletcher, not Asheville. Roughly fifteen miles south of downtown and nine miles north of Hendersonville. If you're setting a pin for a driver, use the terminal address, not "Asheville airport."
  • It's a real regional hub. AVL is among the busiest regional airports in the country and supports thousands of jobs locally. The rebuild isn't vanity; the old building genuinely could not handle the traffic.
  • Groups and luggage. Four people with checked bags do not fit in a standard sedan, and this catches people out constantly. Request the bigger vehicle class, or arrange a van.
  • Late arrivals. Driver supply thins out significantly for the last arrivals of the night. If you're landing after about 10pm, arranging something in advance is the difference between a ten-minute wait and a forty-minute one.
  • Tip your driver on an early run. A 3:45am pickup means somebody got up at 3. It's a genuinely unpleasant shift and it's usually the difference between you making your flight and not.

The part nobody tells you

The drive from AVL to Asheville is short, but it's the first fifteen minutes of your trip and it sets the tone. Coming north on I-26 the mountains stack up on your left, and on a clear morning the ridges go blue all the way out. Most people are on their phones for it.

Look up. That view is the reason you came.
